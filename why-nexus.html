Why Nexus

Building habits that outlast the game.

A repertoire dates and a rating moves every month. How a player thinks when the position is unfamiliar is the part that stays with them.

01

A starting point, not a syllabus

Every student begins with an evaluation game, reviewed by a coach rather than an engine alone. What comes next is shaped by what that game showed — not by whichever module happens to be next in the course.

02

Coaches who stay with you

You work with the same coach week after week, so nothing has to be re-explained. They know which openings you have drilled, which endgames you avoid, and what your clock does under pressure.

03

Understanding over memorisation

Lines you have memorised leave you the moment your opponent deviates. We teach the idea behind the move, so the twentieth move is still yours to find when the book runs out.

04

Every rated game reviewed

Games are only useful if somebody reads them back to you. Yours are annotated and returned with the two or three decisions that actually cost or won the point.

05

Capacity, not enrolment targets

Places are limited by how many students a coach can genuinely follow. When a coach is full, the seat waits rather than the quality dropping.

06

Work that continues between lessons

Opening preparation, daily puzzles, shared study boards and a reading list by level — all of it visible to your coach before the next session starts.
